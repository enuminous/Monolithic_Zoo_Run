# HUMMINGBIRD: prospective integrity surveillance for live experiments

**Matthew Chenoweth Wright**  
Monolithic LLC  
Version 0.1.0 — 2 September 2026

## Abstract

HUMMINGBIRD is a prospective, read-only experiment-integrity monitor and EFMW Zoo Animal #47. It compares the experiment being executed with a frozen specification across protocol, data, information, compute, intervention, and outcome-recording channels. Each observation receives a four-state classification and is committed to an append-only SHA-256 hash chain. The monitor's rules exclude outcome-dependent optimization. HUMMINGBIRD is a falsifiable research proposal rather than evidence for EFMW: it succeeds only if controlled prospective studies show incremental detection of consequential integrity faults over conventional logging and audit at matched information, resource, latency, and false-alarm budgets.

## Design

Let S0 denote the preregistered experiment and St the observed implementation. The system evaluates their deviations, emits an integrity state, and preserves the associated evidence. GREEN denotes encoded conformance, AMBER a qualifying deviation, RED a registered validity breach, and BLACK an uninterpretable evidentiary state.

HUMMINGBIRD has no production write authority. It may observe, timestamp, hash, compare, classify, log, and raise a predetermined flag. Any repair or adaptive change ends the existing evidentiary segment or begins a newly registered phase.

## Prospective firewall

Animals #1–46 form the historical Zoo. HUMMINGBIRD begins Zoo II / ZOOS-OOS-1 and may not be retroactively cited as support for construction of the earlier corpus. Its code, thresholds, fault suite, and failure conditions should be frozen before external evaluation.

## Test

The reference suite injects silent protocol mutation, privileged candidate information, degraded baseline compute, distribution shift, unregistered intervention, benign perturbation, telemetry loss, and outcome temptation. Evaluation against ordinary logging, manual post-hoc audit, and conventional telemetry must measure detection rate, false-alarm rate, latency, overhead, contamination, and severity calibration.

## Failure condition

HUMMINGBIRD fails if a conventional comparator identifies integrity violations equally well or better under matched constraints. A passing software test suite demonstrates implementation conformance only; it does not validate the empirical claim.

## Conclusion

HUMMINGBIRD formalizes a narrow distinction: candidate performance is not identical to procedural integrity, and procedural integrity is not identical to scientific truth. Its canonical rule is: **Hover. Observe. Never secretly steer.**
