# Theory

A final score can be numerically correct while its evidentiary path is invalid. HUMMINGBIRD treats experimental integrity as its own observable process.

Let S0 be the frozen experiment and St the experiment actually executed at time t. HUMMINGBIRD evaluates a channel-specific deviation vector and emits an integrity state.

The monitor does not infer that the candidate is true or false. It estimates whether the frozen comparison remains interpretable.

This creates three intentionally separate objects:

1. candidate performance;
2. experimental conformance;
3. validity of the inference permitted by the evidence chain.

Ordinary monitoring often optimizes system availability or model performance. HUMMINGBIRD instead freezes its integrity rules and evaluates candidate and baselines symmetrically. Its proposed contribution is empirical and falsifiable: consequential experimental faults should be detected with useful sensitivity and latency at tolerable overhead and false-alarm rate.

The architecture remains independently useful even if EFMW fails, because the claim under test concerns procedural integrity rather than EFMW's physical or predictive claims.
